# OSFOTC.001: Fiber Optic Safety, Handling, Inspection, and Cleaning Basics

Fiber-optic technician work begins with safe handling and contamination control before testing or troubleshooting.

## Learning objectives

After this lesson, the learner should be able to:

- explain why an unknown fiber must never be viewed directly;
- control glass-fiber scraps safely;
- identify racks, panels, trays, cassettes, ports, connector types, and circuit labels before disturbing a link;
- protect connector ferrule endfaces from contamination;
- use an inspect-clean-inspect workflow;
- explain why dust caps help but do not prove cleanliness;
- follow manufacturer bend-radius and pulling-tension limits;
- connect poor handling to attenuation and insertion loss;
- use a VFL without treating it as a substitute for loss testing or OTDR analysis;
- prepare clean reference conditions for later optical-power and OLTS measurements;
- document patch-panel work and restore protection after service.

## Core technician sequence

```text
identify circuit
      ↓
make work area safe
      ↓
verify source state
      ↓
protect eyes and hands
      ↓
inspect connector
      ↓
clean if needed
      ↓
inspect again
      ↓
connect
      ↓
route without stress
      ↓
label, test, document
```

## Safety and cleanliness rules

- Never look into the end of an unknown fiber, connector, transceiver, adapter, or test source.
- Put glass scraps directly into an approved closed container.
- Do not touch the connector ferrule endface.
- Do not wipe endfaces on clothing or blow on them.
- Keep unused ports and connectors protected with clean dust caps.
- Use approved inspection scopes and cleaning tools.
- Respect the actual cable manufacturer's minimum bend radius and pulling tension.
- Confirm circuit identity and redundancy state before disconnecting production fiber.

## Key rule
