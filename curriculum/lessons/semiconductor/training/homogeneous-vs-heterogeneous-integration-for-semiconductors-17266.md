<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Homogeneous integration combines similar or identical <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/17/semiconductor-packaging/">semiconductor dies</a> within one package or system. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Examples include multiple memory dies stacked together, repeated processor chiplets, or several dies manufactured with the same technology and designed to perform similar functions. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This approach can improve capacity, scalability, manufacturing flexibility, and performance without requiring one extremely large monolithic chip.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Heterogeneous integration combines dies that perform different functions or use different manufacturing processes. A single package may include <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/15/comparing-cpu-architectures-x64-x86-risc/">CPU</a> chiplets, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/10/01/metered-mining-hosts-gpu-and-asic-hosting-at-wisconsin-mining-facility/">GPU</a> dies, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/05/16/emmc-vs-ufs-embedded-storage-technologies/">high-bandwidth memory</a>, input-output controllers, analog circuits, photonics, or specialized <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/07/29/yes-you-do-want-that-data-center-just-not-in-your-backyard/">artificial intelligence</a> accelerators. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Each component can be manufactured using the process node or material best suited to its purpose before being integrated into one system.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The distinction concerns the types of components being combined, not whether the dies are arranged in two or three dimensions. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A package can therefore be 2D and heterogeneous, 3D and homogeneous, or 3D and heterogeneous. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Heterogeneous integration is becoming increasingly important because it allows semiconductor designers to combine specialized chiplets while balancing performance, cost, power consumption, manufacturing yield, and time to market.</p>
