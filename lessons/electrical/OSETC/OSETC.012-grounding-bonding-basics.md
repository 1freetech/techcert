# OSETC.012: Grounding and Bonding Basics

**Grounding** and **bonding** are related electrical-safety concepts, but they are not the same thing. Grounding connects an electrical system or equipment to earth or another grounding reference. Bonding electrically connects conductive parts so they maintain electrical continuity and substantially the same potential.

This lesson follows **OSETC.011: Lockout/Tagout and Energy Isolation**. It is a recognition-and-theory lesson and does not authorize energized electrical work.

## Grounding in Plain English

Grounding establishes an intentional connection to ground. In properly designed systems, grounding is part of the protection scheme and helps control dangerous voltage conditions.

## Bonding in Plain English

Bonding electrically joins conductive metal parts. Metal enclosures, raceways, and other conductive parts may require effective bonding so continuity is maintained and fault current can be carried safely.

## Why Technicians Care

OSHA requires grounding paths used by circuits, equipment, and enclosures to be permanent and continuous. Bonding conductors used to assure electrical continuity must be able to carry imposed fault current.

## Simple Fault Example

If an energized conductor contacts a metal enclosure, proper equipment grounding and bonding provide an intentional conductive path as part of the system's fault-clearing design.

## Data Center Example

Data centers contain racks, cabinets, cable trays, electrical enclosures, and other conductive structures. Technicians should recognize grounding and bonding conductors and escalate visible damage, looseness, corrosion, or missing connections through site safety procedures.

## Ground Is Not the Same as Neutral

A grounded conductor such as a neutral and an equipment grounding conductor have different functions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Safety Boundary

Do not open energized electrical equipment, alter grounding or bonding connections, or install temporary protective grounds unless qualified, authorized, and following applicable electrical-safety and lockout/tagout procedures.

## Key Takeaway

**Grounding** establishes a connection to ground; **bonding** electrically joins conductive parts. Both are important parts of electrical safety.
