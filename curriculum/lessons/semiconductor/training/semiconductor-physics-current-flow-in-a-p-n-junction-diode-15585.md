<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The current flowing through a p-n junction diode (our one-way gate for electricity) is primarily controlled by four interacting factors. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The most immediate control is the <strong>voltage</strong> applied across the diode: once the push from the battery reaches a certain minimum strength (the turn-on voltage), the current flow <strong>explodes</strong> exponentially, meaning a very small increase in voltage causes a massive jump in current. This flow is extremely sensitive to <strong>temperature</strong>, as heat causes the internal atoms to jiggle, which significantly increases the diode's built-in "leakiness" (the saturation current, I<sub>s</sub>). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Furthermore, temperature also slightly weakens the internal barrier, meaning less voltage is needed to start the explosive flow. Setting the baseline for how much current can flow are two design features: the <strong>diode area</strong> and the <strong>ideality factor</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The <strong>diode area</strong> is simply the size of the junction, acting like the width of a bridge; a wider area allows proportionally more current to cross. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Finally, the <strong>ideality factor</strong> is a quality number (usually between 1 and 2) that accounts for imperfections, such as charges crashing and disappearing (<strong>recombination</strong>) inside the junction; a higher ideality factor means a less perfect, less steep current curve, requiring a bit more voltage to achieve a large current flow.</p>
