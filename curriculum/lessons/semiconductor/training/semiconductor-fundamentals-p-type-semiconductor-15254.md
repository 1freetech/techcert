<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A p-type semiconductor is formed by doping a pure semiconductor, such as silicon, with trivalent elements like boron, gallium, or indium. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>These dopants have only three valence electrons, one fewer than silicon, which creates a vacancy or “hole” in the crystal lattice. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Holes act as positive charge carriers because electrons from neighboring atoms can move to fill them, effectively shifting the vacancy through the lattice. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://youtube.com/shorts/2yC1DqxglIE?si=3NhZDZgYg-UxFfjn","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-9-16 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In p-type materials, holes are the majority carriers, while electrons are the minority carriers. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This unique property allows p-type semiconductors to complement n-type semiconductors in forming p–n junctions, which are the basis of diodes, transistors, and solar cells. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The interaction between electrons and holes in these junctions enables rectification, amplification, and energy conversion. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>P-type semiconductors are therefore critical in designing circuits that control and manipulate electrical signals, forming the foundation of countless electronic devices.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
