# OSETC.015: Relays and Contactors Basics

Relays and contactors are electrically controlled switches. The core technician concept is simple: **a coil controls contacts**.

## The Coil

Applying the correct rated voltage to the coil creates a magnetic field that moves the internal switching mechanism.

## The Contacts

- Normally open (NO): open when de-energized, closes when operated.
- Normally closed (NC): closed when de-energized, opens when operated.

## Relay vs. Contactor

- **Relay:** commonly switches lower-power control or signal circuits.
- **Contactor:** designed for repeated switching of larger electrical loads.

Always use the actual device ratings and diagram.

## Common Contactor Terminals

Many three-pole contactors use L1/L2/L3 for the incoming side, T1/T2/T3 for the load side, and A1/A2 for the coil. These are common conventions; always verify the specific device diagram.

## Simple Example

A thermostat or control system can command a contactor coil rather than carrying the full current of a large motor. Energizing the coil changes the main contact state and allows the engineered power circuit to operate the load.

## Data Center Example

Relays and contactors can appear in cooling, pump, fan, generator, transfer, and other facility-control systems.

## Bitcoin Mining Example

A mining facility can use contactors as part of engineered power or cooling controls, allowing a small control circuit to command larger fans or pumps.

## Safety Boundary

Relays and contactors may be connected to hazardous voltage and high-current loads. Follow equipment documentation, lockout/tagout, PPE requirements, qualified-person boundaries, and site electrical-safety procedures.

## Key Takeaway

Relays and contactors use an electromagnetic coil to operate contacts. Technician fundamentals are recognizing the coil, contacts, common terminal markings, device ratings, and safe troubleshooting boundaries.
