<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Ceva unveiled a <a href="https://www.ceva-ip.com/press/ceva-unveils-next-generation-neupro-npu-portfolio-and-announces-expansion-into-ai-infrastructure/">four-tier NeuPro neural processing unit (NPU) portfolio</a> on October 28, 2026, extending its licensable semiconductor architecture from tiny always-on sensors to large-language-model inference in data centers. The Maryland-based company says the common architecture and software environment could let chipmakers reuse their development work across different power and performance classes.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The four designs are <strong>NeuPro Nano</strong> for low-power sensing, <strong>Prime</strong> for mainstream vision and transformers, <strong>Max</strong> for multimodal edge AI and robotics, and <strong>Ultra</strong> for AI infrastructure. These are processor intellectual-property cores that customers can integrate into custom chips—not four finished processors already shipping to consumers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Performance claims need independent testing</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Ceva claims the new architecture offers <strong>up to twice the AI throughput per square millimeter</strong> and <strong>50% better energy efficiency</strong> than the previous generation. Those are vendor-reported improvements, not independently reproduced benchmarks. The accompanying chart uses a normalized old-generation baseline of 100; it does not imply that any specific application doubles its performance.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":28098,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} --><figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ceva-neupro-2026-up-to-2x-throughput-50-percent-energy-efficiency-indexed-chart.png" alt="Indexed comparison chart: first generation 100, new NeuPro up to 200 in throughput per area and 150 for energy efficiency, according to company claims" class="wp-image-28098" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Indexed company claims from <a href="https://www.ceva-ip.com/press/ceva-unveils-next-generation-neupro-npu-portfolio-and-announces-expansion-into-ai-infrastructure/">Ceva's October 28 announcement</a>: throughput/mm² up to 2× and energy efficiency improved by 50% compared with its first generation. Not third-party measurements.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Technical changes include <strong>FP4 and FP8</strong> low-precision arithmetic, improved tensor utilization, dynamic tensor shapes and <strong>paged key-value caching</strong> for memory-intensive transformer inference. Support for Mixture-of-Experts and linear-attention models is intended to let designers run more varied workloads without replacing the entire NPU architecture.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">From sensors to memory-side inference</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Ultra moves Ceva into a particularly contested market: integrating inference near memory, connectivity and data-processing components. That could reduce some transfers between processors and memory, although actual system savings will depend on a customer's silicon, memory bandwidth and workload. The proposal differs from standalone <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/brainchip-akd1500-raspberry-pi-5-neuromorphic-ai-dev-kit-2026/">BrainChip AKD1500 developer hardware</a> and from cloud-oriented <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/intel-one-command-llm-inference-xeon/">Xeon inference software</a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":28104,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} --><figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/neupro-ultra-memory-and-data-movement-e28094-original-editorial-concept.png" alt="Original illustrative semiconductor system with AI compute blocks and nearby memory, representing the concept of reducing data movement in inference systems" class="wp-image-28104" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Original conceptual illustration of inference compute and memory-side data movement; not an actual NeuPro Ultra chip, product render or published die micrograph.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A unified development kit should help customers scale designs from edge products to larger systems. For comparison, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/15/google-synaptics-low-power-edge-ai-chip/">Google and Synaptics</a> have pursued embedded AI processors, while <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/infineon-physical-ai-robots-cars-data-centers-electronica-2026/">Infineon has emphasized physical AI</a> across robotics, vehicles and infrastructure. A shared vocabulary does not mean the products are interchangeable.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Ceva says selected customers are already evaluating the architecture, with <strong>general availability planned for the second quarter of 2027</strong>. It has not disclosed validated customer-chip shipping volumes, priced implementations or comparable independent energy tests for the new family.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The immediate milestone is customer integration, not a claimed data-center breakthrough. Engineers will need real workload benchmarks, memory traffic measurements, power results and software support before deciding whether NeuPro Ultra makes a meaningful difference to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/optical-transceivers-complete-guide-sfp-qsfp-fiber-links-800g/">AI infrastructure connectivity</a> or on-device inference.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
