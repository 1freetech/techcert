# OSETC.017: Control Circuit Symbols and Ladder Diagrams Basics

A ladder diagram is a simple way to draw and understand an electrical control circuit. It resembles a ladder: two vertical rails with horizontal rungs between them.

## Rails and Rungs

Read a beginner ladder rung from left to right, then move down to the next rung.

## Normally Open Contact

A normally open (NO) contact is open in its normal state and changes state when its associated device operates.

## Normally Closed Contact

A normally closed (NC) contact is closed in its normal state and opens when its associated device operates.

## Coil

A coil represents an output such as a relay or contactor coil. When the conditions to its left are satisfied, the coil can energize and its associated contacts change state.

## Basic START/STOP Rung

A common rung uses a normally closed STOP contact, normally open START contact, overload contact, and contactor coil. START can energize the coil; STOP opens the control path.

## Seal-In Contact

A contact associated with the energized contactor can be placed in parallel with START so the contactor remains energized after the momentary START button is released.

## Diagram vs. Physical Location

Symbols next to each other on a drawing are not necessarily physically adjacent. Use wire numbers, terminal labels, equipment tags, and the approved drawing to locate components.

## Data Center and Mining Examples

Cooling fan and pump starters can use ladder-style control diagrams. Recognizing the symbols helps technicians follow the intended sequence and identify which control condition must be satisfied.

## Safety Boundary

A diagram is a troubleshooting map, not permission to work energized. Follow current approved drawings, equipment documentation, lockout/tagout, PPE, and qualified-person boundaries. Never bypass safety devices merely to complete a rung.

## Practice

1. Identify the two rails.
2. Identify an NO contact.
3. Identify an NC contact.
4. Identify a coil.
5. Explain a seal-in contact.
6. Trace a simple rung left to right and identify the conditions needed to energize the output.

## Key Takeaway

Ladder diagrams organize control circuits into rails and rungs. Technician fundamentals are recognizing NO/NC contacts, coils, overload contacts, and simple START/STOP logic and tracing them systematically.
