<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/14/cmos-vs-bios/">BIOS</a></strong> stands for <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/21/bios-basic-input-output-system-overview/">Basic Input/Output System</a></strong>. It is firmware stored on a chip on the motherboard that <strong>initializes hardware components</strong> and <strong>loads the operating system</strong>. BIOS interfaces are text-based, and users access them by pressing keys like <code>DEL</code>, <code>F2</code>, or <code>ESC</code> during system startup.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/21/bios-basic-input-output-system-overview/">BIOS</a> uses <strong>Legacy Boot</strong>, has limited drive support (up to 2.2 TB), and requires navigating through keyboard-only menus. While still present in some older systems, BIOS is being phased out in favor of UEFI.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">What Is UEFI?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/05/20/uefi-firmware/">UEFI</a></strong>, or <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/21/bios-basic-input-output-system-overview/">Unified Extensible Firmware Interface</a></strong>, is the modern replacement for BIOS. It supports:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Graphical menus with mouse support</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Drives over 2 TB</strong></li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/16/troubleshooting-a-bricked-nerdaxe/">Secure Boot</a></strong> to prevent unauthorized OS loading</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Faster boot times</strong> with more advanced diagnostics</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>UEFI stores its data in <strong>.efi files</strong> on the EFI System Partition (ESP) and includes a Preboot Execution Environment (PXE) for network booting.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Technicians must know how to enter both BIOS and UEFI, enable or disable virtualization, change boot order, and configure security features like Secure Boot and TPM (<a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/09/10/trusted-platform-module-tpm-2/">Trusted Platform Module</a>).</p>
