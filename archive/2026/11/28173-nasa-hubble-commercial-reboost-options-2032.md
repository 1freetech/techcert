<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>NASA asked commercial space companies on October 29, 2026</strong>, how they might lift the aging <strong>Hubble Space Telescope</strong> to a safer orbit, repair it, or guide its eventual disposal. The <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasa-explores-potential-commercial-servicing-for-hubble-telescope/">agency's October 30 announcement</a> describes a <strong>request for information</strong>, not a funded mission or contract award. Companies have until <strong>November 30 at 3 p.m. Eastern</strong> to respond.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://sam.gov/workspace/contract/opp/2ecd9dbf4a3946b4968cc0ebaa75d0b3/view">NASA Goddard notice, 80GSFC27RFIHSTS</a>, asks about capturing Hubble and boosting it to <strong>at least 600 kilometers</strong>, servicing aging equipment, or performing a controlled reentry. Any boost or repair would need to avoid damaging the telescope. NASA anticipates that a possible mission would need to occur <strong>by 2032</strong>; it has not chosen a provider, spacecraft or price.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">What NASA is asking industry to do</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Hubble has no onboard propulsion to maintain its altitude. Thin atmospheric drag slowly lowers its orbit, unlike a navigation satellite executing planned burns such as <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/isro-nvs-03-first-orbit-raising-burn-october-2026-navic/">India's NVS-03</a>. NASA's current analysis places likely natural reentry in <strong>2033–2034</strong> without intervention. Higher altitude could buy scientific time but would introduce rendezvous, capture and orbital-debris risks.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The agency's July 2026 lifetime assessment estimated a <strong>10% cumulative probability of reentry by September 2032</strong> and <strong>50% by December 2033</strong>. These probabilities are forecasts—not scheduled impact dates, nor a promise that Hubble will operate until then. Atmospheric density changes with solar activity, so the timeline remains uncertain. A boost could alter those probabilities, but no approved mission exists.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Historic repairs—and today's engineering limits</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Hubble was launched in <strong>1990</strong> and upgraded during five shuttle servicing missions, last in <strong>2009</strong>. Astronaut <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-marine-mechanic-to-doctor-to-astronaut/">Story Musgrave</a> helped repair its optics in 1993, after the telescope's flawed primary mirror blurred early imagery. The original deployment era also included astronaut <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/13/from-spacewalk-to-challenger-deep/">Kathryn Sullivan</a>. Future servicing would use very different hardware now that the shuttle fleet is retired.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":28169,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} --><figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hubble-first-servicing-spacewalk-musgrave-nasa-1993-public-domain.jpg" alt="Authentic NASA photo of Story Musgrave on the space shuttle robotic arm working on Hubble during its December 1993 first servicing mission" class="wp-image-28169" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Authentic NASA STS-61 servicing spacewalk on December 9, 1993, with <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hubble_First_Servicing_EVA_-_GPN-2000-001085.jpg">Story Musgrave and Jeffrey Hoffman</a>. NASA Johnson Space Center, public domain; not a new planned servicing mission.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A commercial vehicle would need precise navigation, safe grapple points, reliable attitude control and careful operations around an irreplaceable observatory. That engineering challenge resembles broader questions about <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/11/what-chips-have-already-flown-in-space/">long-lived space electronics</a>. NASA previously explored commercial Hubble-reboost possibilities in 2022, but those discussions did not produce an approved rescue mission.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>NASA and ESA continue using Hubble for astronomy, complementing newer telescopes operating at different wavelengths. The next milestone is industry feedback, which may help NASA decide whether to issue an actual solicitation. As with <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/nasa-is-now-taking-bids-for-the-space-stations-that-come-after-the-iss/">commercial space-station procurement</a>, requesting information is not the same as buying a flight. Until a mission is selected, Hubble's scientific future still depends on its aging systems and decaying orbit.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
