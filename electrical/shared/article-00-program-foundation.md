# OSEEC.000: What We Are Building, How We Are Building It, and Why


<h1 class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size:clamp(14px, 0.875rem + ((1vw - 3.2px) * 0.98), 16px);">The BitcoinVersus.Tech Open-Source Electrical Training Program is an independent technical education project built to study, organize, explain, and compare electrical standards and procedures used throughout the world.</h1>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The project is designed around a simple idea: electrical knowledge should not remain trapped inside expensive textbooks, fragmented codebooks, institutional training programs, or specialized professional circles. </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The information already exists. The challenge is gathering it, comparing it, explaining it clearly, and placing it into a structure that people can actually follow.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What We Are Building</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are building an open-source electrical standards training system.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The program will examine electrical terminology, voltage classifications, wiring practices, grounding and bonding, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection, equipment ratings, transformers, switchgear, motors, industrial systems, power distribution, renewable energy, data centers, telecommunications infrastructure, and regional electrical standards.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The goal is not to replace licensed electricians, engineers, inspectors, manufacturers, standards organizations, or local authorities.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The goal is to create a structured body of educational material that helps people understand how these systems work, how standards differ, and how electrical decisions are made across industries and jurisdictions.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each article will focus on a specific subject. Some articles will cover basic concepts. Others will compare national standards, equipment practices, calculations, terminology, installation methods, or regulatory frameworks.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Together, the articles will form a large, searchable technical curriculum.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How We Are Building It</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The program is being built one article at a time.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each article will isolate a specific topic and explain it using clear language, technical references, diagrams, examples, comparisons, and practical applications.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The articles will be organized so that readers can begin with fundamental concepts and gradually move into more advanced systems.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The project will use information from publicly available standards, manufacturer documentation, government publications, engineering references, academic research, technical manuals, and field experience.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Whenever possible, the material will compare multiple systems rather than presenting one country’s electrical practices as universal.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A conductor size, voltage classification, wiring color, grounding method, code requirement, or equipment rating may change depending on location, application, and governing standard. Understanding those differences is a major part of the program.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The project will also remain open to revision.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Electrical systems change. Standards are updated. Technologies evolve. New equipment enters the market. Existing practices are challenged or replaced.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because the program is published digitally, articles can be corrected, expanded, reorganized, and improved over time.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is not intended to be a finished textbook frozen in one edition.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It is an open-source technical knowledge base.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The OSETC Credential</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The program supports the OSESA credential: Open-Source Electrical Technician Certification.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">An OSESA is not automatically a licensed electrician, professional engineer, electrical inspector, or authority having jurisdiction.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The credential represents the study and analysis of electrical standards, systems, terminology, equipment, and regional practices through the BitcoinVersus.Tech program.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The emphasis is on research, comparison, documentation, technical literacy, and the ability to communicate electrical concepts accurately.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The credential is intended to demonstrate that the participant completed a structured program of independent electrical standards education and analysis.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Are We Doing This?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because the electrical industry is massive, fragmented, technical, and increasingly important.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modern civilization depends on electrical infrastructure.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Homes, hospitals, factories, telecommunications networks, transportation systems, data centers, mining facilities, renewable energy systems, utilities, and nearly every digital technology depend on reliable electrical power.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yet much of the information surrounding those systems is difficult to access, difficult to understand, or separated across thousands of documents and organizations.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is value in bringing that information together.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is value in explaining it clearly.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is value in comparing systems across countries and industries.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is value in creating a public record of the research.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">There is also value in proving that serious technical education does not have to begin with institutional permission.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why Us?</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Because we can.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We have access to the tools, information, publishing infrastructure, and technical experience necessary to begin.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We do not need to wait for a university, corporation, government agency, training provider, or standards organization to decide that this project should exist.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The internet allows independent researchers to collect information, publish analysis, build educational systems, and improve them publicly.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That does not mean the work will always be perfect.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It means the work can begin.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The project will develop through research, criticism, correction, experimentation, and continued publication.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some articles may be revised many times. Some topics may require deeper investigation. Some conclusions may change when better information becomes available.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is not a weakness.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That is how an open technical project is supposed to work.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Mission</h2>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The mission of the OSESA Project is to make electrical standards education more accessible, comparative, practical, and globally informed.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are building a technical library for people who want to understand electrical infrastructure beyond memorized definitions and isolated code requirements.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are studying the systems that power the modern world.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are documenting what we learn.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">We are publishing it openly.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">And we are doing it because we can.</p>
