OSETC.003 continues the technician meter sequence with resistance and continuity testing.

## Resistance
Resistance describes opposition to electric current and is measured in ohms (Ω). Resistance measurements are performed with the circuit de-energized.

## Continuity
Continuity mode provides a quick indication of a sufficiently low-resistance path between two points. It is useful for tracing wiring and checking fuses, switches, and conductors, but a continuity indication alone does not prove correct operation under load.

## De-energize before resistance testing
Do not connect a meter in resistance or continuity mode to an energized circuit. Follow the applicable workplace energy-control procedure and verify the circuit condition before testing. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333 provides requirements for electrical safety-related work practices and verification of de-energized conditions for covered work.

## Basic meter setup
With the circuit safely de-energized, inspect the meter and leads. Typically, place the black lead in COM and the red lead in the resistance-capable input. Select Ω or continuity as appropriate.

## Interpreting a measurement
Very low resistance can indicate a continuous conductive path. Very high or over-range resistance can indicate an open path. Compare measurements with schematics, specifications, known-good equipment, or manufacturer documentation.

## Technician exercise
1. Use only a safe, isolated training circuit or loose components.
2. Inspect the meter and leads.
3. Select continuity and touch the probes together to observe the response.
4. Test a known-good fuse or short conductor.
5. Switch to resistance mode and record its resistance.
6. Compare a continuous path with an intentionally open path.

## OSET takeaway
Voltage testing asks what electrical potential exists between two points. Resistance and continuity testing examine the electrical path itself. Selecting the correct measurement and establishing a safe circuit condition first are core technician skills.
