<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>When <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/20/comptia-a-troubleshooting-methodology/">troubleshooting</a> motherboards, RAM, CPU, and power issues, the first step is to carefully <strong>observe system behavior</strong> during startup. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Common symptoms like no <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/08/understanding-ups-devices-uninterruptible-power-supply/">power</a>, random reboots, beep codes, no display, or failure to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/10/post-power-on-self-test/">POST (Power-On Self-Test)</a> often indicate problems with these core components.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>For power issues, always verify that the <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2024/03/01/computer-muscle-understanding-the-power-supply-unit/">power supply unit</a> (PSU)</strong> is delivering correct voltages using a multimeter or power supply tester. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Loose power connectors, a faulty power button, or an overloaded power strip can also cause no-power scenarios. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXEVsKl57bE","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

</div></figure>
<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>For motherboards, look for physical signs of damage like <strong>bulging capacitors</strong>, <strong>burnt components</strong>, or <strong>discoloration</strong>. Resetting the <strong>CMOS battery</strong> or <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/21/bios-basic-input-output-system-overview/">BIOS</a></strong> to default can sometimes resolve <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/10/post-power-on-self-test/">POST failures</a> caused by corrupted firmware settings.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":"","style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">RAM-related problems usually present as <strong>system freezes</strong>, <strong>random restarts</strong>, or <strong>beep codes</strong> specific to memory errors. Troubleshooting steps include reseating the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/28/installing-the-appropriate-ram/">RAM</a>, trying one stick at a time, or swapping slots to identify faulty modules or DIMM slots. Using memory diagnostic tools like <strong>MemTest86</strong> helps confirm if RAM is defective. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":"","style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">CPU problems are less common but can occur due to <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/16/bitaxe-gamma-601-device-overheat-mode-standard-procedure-for-resolving-bitaxe-miner-overheat-alerts/">overheating</a></strong>, improper installation, or <strong>bent pins</strong> (on PGA sockets) or <strong>socket damage</strong>. Ensuring proper thermal paste application and a functional cooling system is vital to prevent CPU shutdowns or thermal throttling. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":"","style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">When all else fails, testing the suspect components on another known-good system, or replacing with known-good parts, is often necessary to isolate the failing hardware.</p>
