## Purpose

Article 2 moves from the codes-and-jurisdictions framework into a fundamental field concept: equipment must be properly isolated and its electrical condition verified before it is treated as de-energized.

This material is educational and does not authorize unqualified electrical work. Applicable law, employer procedures, site rules, equipment instructions, and qualified-person requirements control actual work.

## Off is not the same as electrically safe

A switch or breaker in the OFF position does not by itself prove every exposed circuit part is de-energized. Backfeed, induced voltage, labeling errors, stored energy, or an incorrect disconnect can leave hazards present.

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333 requires safety-related work practices intended to prevent electrical injury and generally requires exposed live parts to be de-energized before work unless a recognized exception applies.

## Safety sequence

1. Identify relevant energy sources.
2. Follow the approved shutdown procedure.
3. Isolate the equipment.
4. Apply required lockout/tagout controls.
5. Perform required operating-control checks.
6. A qualified person performs the required electrical verification with appropriate test equipment.
7. Treat the equipment as de-energized only after the applicable procedure is complete.

## Why testing matters

OSHA requires test equipment to verify that circuit elements and electrical parts to which workers will be exposed are de-energized. Verification also addresses unexpected conditions such as induced voltage or backfeed.

A status light can be useful information, but OSHA has explained that an indicator does not replace required test-equipment verification.

## Live-dead-live concept

A common professional approach verifies the tester on a known source, tests for absence of voltage, and verifies the tester again. The principle is that a zero reading is useful only when the instrument itself is known to be functioning.

## Data-center example

An open breaker and equipment label may suggest that a downstream circuit is off. The safe-work process does not stop at the label: the correct energy source must be identified, isolated and controlled under the site's procedure, followed by the required qualified-person verification.

## Knowledge check

1. Does an OFF breaker alone prove exposed parts are safe? No.
2. Why can backfeed matter? Another source can energize a circuit from the load side.
3. Why verify the tester around an absence-of-voltage test? To establish that the instrument is functioning.
4. Who performs the OSHA electrical verification for exposed circuit parts? A qualified person.
5. Does this course replace licensing, qualification, or site procedures? No.
