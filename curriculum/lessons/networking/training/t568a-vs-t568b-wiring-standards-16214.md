<p>T568A and T568B are two valid Ethernet wiring standards that differ only in how the green and orange wire pairs are arranged, but this small change matters when matching existing infrastructure or ensuring consistent terminations. Both are approved under ANSI/TIA‑568.2‑D and will function identically as long as both ends of a cable use the same pattern.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>T568A is technically the preferred standard for new installations because it offers better backward compatibility with older telephone wiring, while T568B became more common in commercial networks due to historical adoption by certain vendors. Functionally, neither provides a performance advantage; the key is consistency. </p>

<p>Mixing A on one end and B on the other creates a crossover cable, which is only appropriate in specific legacy scenarios. For modern structured cabling, choose the standard already used in your environment and stick to it throughout the installation to avoid troubleshooting headaches.</p>
