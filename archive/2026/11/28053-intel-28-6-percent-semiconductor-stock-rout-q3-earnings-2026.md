<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>Intel shares plunged 28.6% on Friday, October 30, 2026</strong>, after the U.S. chipmaker issued fourth-quarter sales guidance below Wall Street expectations, helping trigger a broad semiconductor selloff. The retreat followed Intel’s October 29 earnings report, which showed third-quarter revenue of <strong>$16.5 billion</strong>, up 21% from a year earlier. Investors were reacting principally to the outlook, not an outright decline in completed-quarter sales.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In its <a href="https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1786/intel-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results">official earnings release</a>, Intel forecast <strong>$15 billion to $16 billion</strong> of fourth-quarter revenue. Analysts’ average estimate was approximately $17.07 billion, <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/intel-warning-sparks-chip-rout-pc-weakness-clouds-turnaround-2026-10-30/">Reuters reported</a>. Management also warned of continued cash consumption as manufacturing expansion and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/10/pc-shipments-plunge-20-1-q3-2026-memory-costs-ai-data-centers/">slowing PC demand</a> pressured the business.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Data-center growth contrasts with weaker PCs</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The October 30 market losses were not uniform: Intel declined <strong>28.6%</strong>, AMD <strong>10.9%</strong> and Nvidia <strong>4.2%</strong>. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.2%, according to <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/nasdaq-futures-tumble-amazon-intel-results-deepen-ai-spending-woes-2026-10-30/">Reuters’ closing-market report</a>. These are comparable <strong>one-session stock returns</strong>, not changes in physical semiconductor production or dollar-valued market capitalization.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Intel’s divisions told a more complicated story. <strong>Data Center and AI</strong> revenue jumped 78% to <strong>$7.3 billion</strong>, while its PC-oriented Client Computing and Physical AI division slipped 5% to <strong>$8.1 billion</strong>. Segment and foundry figures cannot simply be added together because Intel eliminates transactions between its businesses when calculating consolidated revenue.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Demand for <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/intel-one-command-llm-inference-xeon/">Xeon server processors</a> remained strong, but supply constraints limited shipments. Intel also faces the separately reported <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/intel-kiryat-gat-fab28-explosion-two-week-shutdown-xeon-revenue-2026/">Kiryat Gat factory disruption</a>, which Reuters said could cost approximately $600 million in revenue across two quarters. That production event is related to the outlook but is not the same news as Friday’s stock-market reaction.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Manufacturing and cash flow remain the test</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Intel continues investing in advanced process technology while attempting to bring factories and customer demand into balance. Its 18A manufacturing work, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/asml-ai-demand-outrunning-supply/">industrywide lithography demand</a> and the rising cost of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/28/sk-hynix-q3-2026-104-trillion-won-hbm4-ai-memory-profit/">high-bandwidth memory</a> illustrate why strong demand can coexist with strained supply chains.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
