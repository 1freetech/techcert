<p><strong>LCD (Liquid Crystal Display)</strong> and <strong>OLED (Organic Light Emitting Diode)</strong> technologies are two widely used screen types in modern devices, each with distinct characteristics. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>LCDs require a <strong>backlight</strong> to illuminate pixels, which means blacks can appear grayish, but they offer consistent brightness and are generally more affordable. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>OLED displays, on the other hand, emit their own light per pixel, resulting in <strong>true blacks</strong>, <strong>higher contrast</strong>, and <strong>more vibrant colors</strong>, especially in darker scenes. OLEDs are common in smartphones and high-end displays but may suffer from <strong>burn-in</strong> over time, whereas LCDs are more durable in static-use environments like office monitors.</p>
