<p><strong>VGA (Video Graphics Array)</strong> and <strong>DisplayPort</strong> are two video output standards used to connect computers to monitors or projectors, but they differ drastically in technology, image quality, and capability. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>VGA</strong>, introduced in the late 1980s, is an <strong>analog interface</strong> that supports resolutions up to 1920×1080, but often suffers from <strong>signal degradation</strong> over long cable runs. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>VGA uses a <strong>15-pin D-sub connector</strong> and does not carry audio, making it a legacy option mostly found in older monitors and projectors. Its analog nature makes it more vulnerable to interference, and it lacks support for modern high-definition content protection (HDCP).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>DisplayPort</strong>, on the other hand, is a <strong>digital standard</strong> developed for high-performance video transmission. It supports <strong>resolutions up to 8K</strong>, <strong>multi-stream transport (MST)</strong>, and can transmit <strong>audio and video simultaneously</strong> through a single cable. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>DisplayPort also supports <strong>adaptive sync technologies</strong> like FreeSync and G-Sync, making it ideal for gaming and professional graphics work. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The connector is compact, has a latch to secure connections, and includes backward compatibility with HDMI via adapters.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
