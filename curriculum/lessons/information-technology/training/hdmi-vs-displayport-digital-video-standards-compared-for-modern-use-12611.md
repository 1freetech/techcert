<p><strong>HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface)</strong> and <strong>DisplayPort</strong> are two widely adopted digital display interfaces, but they are optimized for different environments. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>HDMI</strong> is the standard for <strong>consumer electronics</strong>, including TVs, projectors, game consoles, and media players. It supports both <strong>audio and video</strong> in a single cable and includes features like <strong>CEC (Consumer Electronics Control)</strong> and <strong>ARC (Audio Return Channel)</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>HDMI is backward compatible and supports multiple versions, with <strong>HDMI 2.1</strong> allowing resolutions up to <strong>8K at 60Hz</strong> and dynamic HDR support.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>DisplayPort</strong>, developed by VESA, is geared toward <strong>computer and professional environments</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>It supports higher bandwidths than HDMI at comparable versions, and includes <strong>Multi-Stream Transport (MST)</strong>, which allows daisy-chaining multiple monitors from a single port. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>DisplayPort 1.4 supports up to <strong>8K at 60Hz</strong> with <strong>Display Stream Compression (DSC)</strong>, while <strong>DisplayPort 2.0</strong> increases bandwidth for even higher refresh rates and color depths. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Unlike HDMI, DisplayPort doesn’t carry licensing fees, making it more common in enterprise and OEM hardware. For technicians, understanding which to recommend depends on <strong>device compatibility, refresh rate needs, and feature support</strong>, particularly in multi-monitor workstations or home theater builds.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
