OSET.004 introduces electrical current and clamp-meter measurement.

## Current means flow
Current is the flow of electric charge and is measured in amperes (A). Loads draw current while operating.

## Why clamp meters are useful
A clamp meter senses the magnetic field around a conductor, allowing current measurement without inserting a conventional meter directly in series when the instrument and procedure are appropriate.

## Clamp one conductor
For ordinary load-current measurement, clamp around one conductor. Clamping outgoing and returning conductors together can cancel their magnetic fields and create an incorrect or near-zero reading.

## AC and DC current
Use an instrument designed for the current type and expected magnitude. DC-capable clamps may require zeroing before measurement.

## Compare readings with expected load
Compare measurements with equipment nameplates, breaker ratings, normal baselines, or other phases to identify overload, underloading, imbalance, or abnormal behavior.

## Technician exercise
1. Identify the current and CAT ratings of a clamp meter.
2. Locate AC-current and, if available, DC-current functions.
3. On an appropriate safe training setup, identify the single conductor to place inside the jaws.
4. Close the jaws completely and observe the reading.
5. Compare measured current with the expected load.

## OSET takeaway
Voltage describes electrical potential, resistance describes opposition to current, and current describes charge flow. Clamp meters provide a practical way to measure current when properly selected and used.
