# OSETC.018: Interlocks and Permissives Basics

An interlock or permissive adds a condition to an electrical control circuit.

## Permissive

A permissive is a condition that must be satisfied before an output is allowed to operate. For example, Motor 2 may be allowed to start only after Motor 1 is running.

## Interlock

An interlock prevents an operation when another condition makes it unsafe, undesirable, or mechanically incompatible.

## Two-Motor Example

Fan 2 can require proof that Fan 1 is running. An auxiliary contact associated with Fan 1 can become a permissive in Fan 2's control rung.

## Forward and Reverse Example

Separate forward and reverse contactors can be interlocked so both cannot be energized at the same time.

## Permissive vs. Seal-In

A permissive establishes a required condition. A seal-in contact commonly maintains a control circuit after a momentary START command.

## Data Center and Mining Examples

Cooling pumps and fans may require other approved conditions before they can run. A technician can trace the ladder diagram to determine which condition is preventing operation.

## Troubleshooting Mindset

If an output does not energize, identify every condition in the approved rung before assuming a component has failed. An active interlock or unsatisfied permissive may be correctly preventing operation.

## Safety Boundary

Never bypass interlocks, permissives, overloads, emergency stops, or other protective conditions merely to make equipment run. Follow approved drawings, equipment documentation, lockout/tagout, PPE, and qualified-person boundaries.

## Practice

1. Define a permissive.
2. Define an interlock.
3. Explain why Motor 2 may require Motor 1 to run first.
4. Distinguish a permissive from a seal-in contact.
5. Trace a simple rung and identify required conditions.
6. Explain why bypassing an interlock is not a normal troubleshooting step.

## Key Takeaway

Permissives establish conditions required before operation; interlocks prevent conflicting or unwanted operations. Trace each condition systematically rather than guessing.
