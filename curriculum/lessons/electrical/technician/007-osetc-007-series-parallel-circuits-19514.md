<!-- wp:paragraph --><p><strong>OSETC.007</strong> introduces two basic ways electrical components can be connected: <strong>series</strong> and <strong>parallel</strong>. The easiest difference to remember is the number of paths available for current.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Series means one path</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In a simple series circuit, components are connected one after another. Current has one path through the circuit. If that path opens, current stops through the series circuit.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>For resistors in series, total resistance is simple: <strong>R total = R1 + R2 + R3</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Parallel means multiple paths</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>In a parallel circuit, branches provide more than one path for current. Components connected across the same two nodes have the same voltage across those nodes.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Parallel paths also matter during meter testing. Fluke notes that other components connected in parallel can affect an in-circuit resistance reading, usually lowering the measured total resistance. <a href="https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/digital-multimeters/how-to-measure-resistance">See Fluke’s resistance-measurement guide.</a></p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Simple example</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Put two 10 Ω resistors in series and their resistance adds: <strong>10 Ω + 10 Ω = 20 Ω</strong>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Put two equal 10 Ω resistors in parallel and the total resistance becomes <strong>5 Ω</strong>. At this stage, the important idea is simply that adding parallel paths can reduce total resistance.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Quick practice</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} --><ol class="wp-block-list"><li>Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 5 Ω are in series. Total resistance = <strong>10 Ω</strong>.</li><li>A circuit has one continuous path. It is a <strong>series</strong> circuit.</li><li>A circuit has several branches across the same two nodes. It is a <strong>parallel</strong> circuit.</li><li>Before measuring resistance, de-energize the circuit and consider whether parallel paths could affect the reading.</li></ol><!-- /wp:list -->
<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">OSETC takeaway</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Remember: <strong>series = one path</strong> and <strong>parallel = multiple paths</strong>. Recognizing the circuit arrangement helps a technician understand measurements and find opens, branches, and unexpected resistance readings.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
