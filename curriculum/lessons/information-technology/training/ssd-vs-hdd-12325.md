<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/29/overview-of-storage-devices/">Solid-State Drives</a> (SSDs)</strong> and <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/01/07/command-8-diskpart-windows-os/">Hard Disk Drives</a> (HDDs)</strong> are two primary forms of internal and external storage used in desktops, laptops, and servers, each with distinct characteristics. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/27/m-2-overview/">SSDs</a></strong> store data on <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/29/overview-of-storage-devices/">flash memory</a> chips, offering <strong>lightning-fast read/write speeds</strong>, lower latency, and greater durability due to the absence of moving parts. They’re ideal for operating systems, boot drives, and applications requiring fast access, often using SATA or <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/02/27/m-2-overview/">NVMe</a> interfaces. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>HDDs</strong>, on the other hand, store data magnetically on spinning platters and use a mechanical read/write arm. While they are <strong>significantly slower</strong> than SSDs, they offer <strong>larger storage capacities at a lower cost</strong>, making them a go-to for bulk storage like backups and media libraries.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
