OSEEC.029 introduces a dangerous misunderstanding in industrial motor control: Safe Torque Off (STO) inhibits torque-producing energy in a compatible drive, but does not electrically isolate its power terminals. A stopped shaft, cleared alarm or dark display is not proof that a machine is safe for electrical work. The lesson follows OSEEC.028 on dual-channel safety relays, extending the safety chain to the variable-frequency drive (VFD). Siemens' official STO reference distinguishes torque inhibition from electrical isolation.

How Safe Torque Off works

A VFD creates a controlled motor output by switching semiconductor devices. Its certified STO function prevents the output stage from applying the switching sequence needed to produce commanded motor torque. On drives using a stop-category-0 strategy, STO results in coasting rather than a commanded ramp to zero speed. Mechanical inertia may keep a fan, pump or conveyor moving after the safety demand. For VFD conversion and its DC bus, see OSEEC.018 on drive engineering.

STO is standardized as a drive functional-safety function under IEC 61800-5-2. Actual safety integrity depends on the certified drive, wiring or network architecture, diagnostic coverage, application risk assessment and validation. A drive with an STO label does not automatically make an entire machine compliant. Rockwell's January 2026 PowerFlex 750 safety manual specifically warns that STO is not electrical safety and that power-device failures can create residual movement.

STO is not an electrical disconnect

A safety demand may originate at an emergency-stop button or another protective device. STO can serve as the final torque-inhibiting action, but it does not necessarily remove supply voltage, discharge the DC link, or eliminate other stored energy. Qualified workers must follow the equipment-specific electrical isolation, lockout/tagout and absence-of-voltage verification procedure before electrical service. See OSETC.011 on lockout/tagout.

A controlled safety stop, when supported and engineered, can decelerate a machine before reaching a defined safe state. STO alone generally allows coasting. A suspended or externally driven load may still move under gravity or mechanical force, which can require a rated brake or other safeguards. Neither a safety-status indication nor a quiet motor demonstrates that access hazards have been eliminated.

Two channels and safe restart are system questions

Many STO implementations use redundant safety channels or certified safety communications, but wiring count alone does not establish Performance Level or Safety Integrity Level. Engineers must assess sensor diagnostics, faults, reaction time, feedback and the complete safety-related control system. OSEEC.027 on mirror contacts explains why observing the final switching element can be important in related safety functions.

Releasing an emergency-stop or clearing an STO demand should not be treated as permission to restart machinery. Depending on the risk assessment, the design must require an appropriate reset and distinct deliberate start action. The engineer also needs to know how input-channel faults, drive inhibit status and unexpected restart conditions are detected and logged.

Read-only practice: investigate a conveyor

Imagine a hypothetical conveyor that receives an emergency-stop demand. Its safety system activates STO. The drive reports that torque is inhibited, and the belt coasts to a standstill. A technician proposes opening the live VFD enclosure because the motor no longer turns. Identify the missing safety evidence before any access.

The correct engineering review asks for a defined stopping-time assessment, residual motion analysis, verified guarding and access times, restart behavior, documented fault response and authorized electrical-isolation process. The technician must not substitute an STO indication for absence-of-voltage verification. No jumper, wiring changes, drive tests or energized measurements are part of the exercise.

How to review an STO design

Start with the machine risk assessment and required level of risk reduction. Verify the exact drive model and approved safety function, the complete signal path, reaction and stopping time, diagnostic treatment of failures, and the validated restart sequence. Compare all claims with the applicable manufacturer documentation rather than copying a circuit from another product family. The Rockwell manual also cautions that STO must not replace ordinary operational starting and stopping controls.

Takeaway: STO prevents commanded motor torque. It does not equal a holding brake, emergency power disconnect or lockout/tagout. A safe machine depends on the validated system, not a single indicator.
