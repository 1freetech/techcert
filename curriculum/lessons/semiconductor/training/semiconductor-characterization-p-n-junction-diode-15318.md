<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A <strong>p-n junction diode</strong> is the simplest form of a semiconductor electronic device, created by joining a <strong>p-type</strong> and an <strong>n-type</strong> semiconductor material within a single crystal.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The p-type material is doped with acceptor impurities, resulting in an excess of <strong>holes</strong> (positive charge carriers), while the n-type material is doped with donor impurities, resulting in an excess of free electrons (negative charge carriers). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the moment of contact, the majority carriers near the interface diffuse across the boundary—electrons from the n-side move to the p-side, and holes from the p-side move to the n-side—and recombine. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This recombination process leaves behind immobile, positively charged donor ions on the n-side and immobile, negatively charged acceptor ions on the p-side, forming a region devoid of mobile charge carriers called the depletion region or space-charge region. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":15327,"sizeSlug":"large","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/pn-junction-diode.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-15327" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><sup><sub><strong>The P-N Junction: The Heart of the Diode</strong> When p-type (rich in <strong>holes</strong>[+]) and n-type (rich in <strong>electrons</strong> [-]) semiconductors meet, a central area called the <strong>depletion region</strong> forms.<sup>3</sup> This region is created by electrons and holes near the boundary recombining, leaving behind fixed, immobile ions (the positive and negative charges shown).<sup>4</sup> This creates an internal electric field and a potential barrier that prevents further flow, defining the diode's essential one-way function. </sub></sup><br><em><sup><sub>Image Credit: Shutterstock</sub></sup> </em></figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This fixed charge creates an internal electric field that establishes a potential barrier across the junction, which prevents further majority carrier flow and gives the diode its essential functionality. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The p-n junction's defining characteristic is its ability to allow electric current to flow effectively in only one direction (forward bias) while largely blocking it in the reverse direction (reverse bias), making it useful for applications like rectification, which converts alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
