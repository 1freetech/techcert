# History of Money, Technology and Bitcoin — Financial Crisis Event Law

Effective: October 29, 2026.
Scope: All future lessons in the active History of Money, Technology and Bitcoin certification rotation (OSHistory), including publication on BitcoinVersus.Tech and its TechCert/Open CERT archives.
Authority: User's direct editorial instruction made during OSHistory.015 preparation.

## Required subject for every new lesson

Each new history-of-money lesson **must be anchored to a real, historically documented event** involving at least one of:
- a bank failure, insolvency, liquidation, banking panic, or bank run;
- a documented market crash or severe market contraction;
- a specific financial panic, credit crisis, financial-market bust or comparable identifiable episode of financial distress.

Do not create a new History lesson whose primary story is only general monetary mechanics, accounting, or an economic concept without an identifiable financial failure or crash. Such background concepts may appear as supporting explanations of the documented event.

## Recurring historical-cycle analysis

For the chosen incident, explain what the evidence supports about:
- preceding expansion, credit growth or boom conditions (if documented);
- warning signs, exposure, leverage, debt, liquidity, governance, confidence and/or political triggers;
- the exact nature, geography, extent and chronology of the failure or contraction;
- transmission to counterparties, markets or communities, only where documented;
- contemporaneous response, restructuring, recovery or subsequent expansion, where supported by the record;
- lessons for institutions and recordkeeping, without false claims that different monetary regimes are mechanically identical.

A **bank failure** does not automatically prove a broader stock-market crash or economy-wide depression. Distinguish individual banking-company liquidation from banking-system panic and a broad market price collapse. Do not fabricate indices, price drawdowns, lost assets, default figures or dates.

Where a proposed historical era has no adequately documented crash or failure, choose another episode and explain its place in the rotation rather than force a speculative crisis.
