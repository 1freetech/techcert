<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Near-End Crosstalk (NEXT) is a type of signal interference that occurs when a transmitted signal on one wire pair induces unwanted noise onto an adjacent pair at the same end of the cable where the signal originated.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>NEXT is most commonly encountered in twisted-pair cabling systems, such as those used in Ethernet networks. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It happens when the electromagnetic field generated by a signal on one pair (the disturbing pair) couples into another pair (the disturbed pair) at the transmitter end.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This interference can corrupt data transmission, especially in high-speed networks like Gigabit Ethernet, where signal integrity is critical.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The severity of NEXT is influenced by factors such as cable quality, twist tightness, termination practices, and signal frequency. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Poorly manufactured cables, excessive untwisting during termination, or improper connector installation can increase susceptibility to NEXT. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Higher-frequency signals are more prone to this type of crosstalk due to their stronger electromagnetic fields and shorter wavelengths.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>To quantify NEXT, technicians measure the difference in signal strength between the transmitted signal and the induced noise at the near end. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Standards like TIA-568 define acceptable NEXT thresholds for different cable categories. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Power Sum NEXT (PSNEXT) is a more rigorous metric that accounts for cumulative interference from all adjacent pairs, and is essential for certifying cables for high-speed applications.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
