<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Insertion Loss in <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/11/06/fiber-optic-training-fiber-connectors-2/">fiber optics</a> refers to the amount of signal power lost when a component—such as a connector, splice, or device—is inserted into the optical path. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It’s measured in decibels (dB), and lower values indicate better performance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When light travels through a <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/07/fiber-optic-cabling/">fiber</a>, any interruption or interface can cause some of the signal to be absorbed, scattered, or misaligned. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Insertion Loss quantifies that drop in power between the input and output of the component. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PqWutMgU2AI\u0026amp;pp=ygUUZmliZXIgSW5zZXJ0aW9uIExvc3M%3D","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For example, a good connector might have an insertion loss of less than 0.3 dB, while a poor splice or dirty interface could exceed 1 dB. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Minimizing insertion loss is essential for maintaining signal strength, especially in long-distance or <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/05/27/wi-fi-standards-and-frequency-bands/">high-bandwidth</a> systems.</p>
