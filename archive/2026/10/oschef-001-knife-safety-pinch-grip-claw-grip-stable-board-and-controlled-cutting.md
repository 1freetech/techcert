<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>A chef’s knife is safest when the cook controls the knife, the food, and the cutting surface at the same time.</strong> Before learning dice sizes, julienne, sharpening, or speed, learn the basic safety system: use a stable cutting board, hold the knife securely, keep the guide-hand fingertips out of the blade path, and move the blade deliberately. The <a href="https://www.ciachef.edu/blog/5-knife-skills-every-chef-must/">Culinary Institute of America</a> treats knife skills as foundational because good technique improves both safety and consistency.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Use a Pinch Grip for Control</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For a typical chef’s knife, a common professional grip is the <strong>pinch grip</strong>. Pinch the blade gently near the handle between the thumb and the side of the index finger, then let the remaining fingers wrap around the handle. The goal is not to squeeze as hard as possible; it is to keep the knife aligned with the hand so the blade responds predictably. The <a href="https://www.ice.edu/blog/knife-skills-video">Institute of Culinary Education</a> emphasizes grip, body position, elbow position, and follow-through as part of one controlled system.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Make the Guide Hand a Claw</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The non-knife hand controls the food. Curl the fingertips inward so the knuckles sit farther forward than the fingertips; this is the <strong>claw grip</strong>. The flat side of the knife can then move near the knuckles while the fingertips remain behind them. Move the guide hand backward gradually as the food gets smaller. Do not flatten the fingertips on top of food near the blade, and do not rush the hand movement to keep up with the knife.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Stabilize the Board and Cut Deliberately</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A cutting board that slides is a safety problem because the food and blade can move unexpectedly. Put the board on a flat surface and use a damp towel or another approved nonslip layer underneath when needed. Keep the work area clear, cut away from your body, and use a smooth controlled rocking or forward slicing motion appropriate to the ingredient. Speed is never the first objective. If the board shifts, the food rolls, the knife grip changes, or your attention breaks, stop and reset before continuing.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Beginner Practice</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><li>Place a cutting board on the counter and verify that it cannot slide before touching the knife.</li><li>Practice the pinch grip with the knife stationary. Confirm that the thumb and index finger control the blade near the handle without excessive squeezing.</li><li>With the knife set safely aside, practice forming the claw grip with the guide hand on a whole carrot or onion.</li><li>Cut a stable vegetable slowly into several pieces while keeping the guide fingertips behind the knuckles.</li><li>Stop after every few cuts and check the board, grip, blade path, and guide-hand position.</li></ol>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Knowledge Check + Answers</strong></h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Why use a pinch grip?</strong> It gives the hand more direct control over the blade and helps the knife move predictably.</li><li><strong>What is the purpose of the claw grip?</strong> It keeps the guide-hand fingertips behind the knuckles and out of the normal blade path.</li><li><strong>What should you do if the cutting board moves?</strong> Stop cutting and stabilize the board before continuing.</li><li><strong>Is speed an important beginner goal?</strong> No. Control, consistency, and safety come first; speed develops later through correct repetition.</li><li><strong>Where should your attention be while cutting?</strong> On the blade path, guide hand, food stability, and cutting surface.</li><li><strong>When should you stop and reset?</strong> Whenever the board shifts, food becomes unstable, the grip changes, or your attention is interrupted.</li></ol>
