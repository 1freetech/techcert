<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Polarization Mode Dispersion (PMD) occurs when light traveling through a single-mode <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/11/10/fiber-optic-theory/">optical fiber</a> splits into two orthogonal polarization components that propagate at slightly different velocities. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This phenomenon is caused by random imperfections, asymmetries, and external stresses in the fiber, such as core ellipticity, bending, or temperature fluctuations.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In an ideal <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/07/fiber-optic-cabling/">fiber</a>, both polarization modes would travel at the same speed. However, real-world fibers exhibit birefringence, causing a differential group delay (DGD) between the modes. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This delay leads to pulse spreading, which degrades signal integrity—especially in high-speed networks (<a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/21/rj45-vs-fiber-optic-comparing-copper-and-optical-networking-media/">10 Gbps</a> and above) and long-haul transmissions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>PMD is statistical and time-varying, making it difficult to predict or fully compensate. It becomes a critical factor in system design for 40 Gbps, 100 Gbps, and higher-speed optical networks.</p>
