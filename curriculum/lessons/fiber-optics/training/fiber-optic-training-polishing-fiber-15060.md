<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Polishing <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/11/07/fiber-optic-training-cleaving-fiber/">fiber</a><em> </em>is the final stage in preparing an optical fiber connector, ensuring a smooth, scratch-free end face that allows for minimal signal loss and optimal light transmission. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The procedure begins after <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/11/07/fiber-optic-training-cleaving-fiber/">the fiber has been cleaved</a> and secured in a connector ferrule using epoxy or a mechanical fit. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once the adhesive cures, the protruding fiber tip is carefully trimmed and polished to achieve a mirror-like surface. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Technicians typically use a fiber polishing machine or a hand polishing puck along with polishing films of varying grit sizes, starting with coarse (typically 12 μm) and progressing to ultra-fine (0.3 μm or finer) diamond lapping films. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The process is performed on a flat glass polishing plate, often covered with a rubber pad to maintain even pressure. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A polishing fixture holds the connector at a precise angle—usually 0° for flat polish or 8° for angled physical contact (APC) connectors. Deionized water or alcohol may be applied to the polishing surface to reduce friction and remove debris. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once polishing is complete, the connector end face is inspected under a fiber microscope or interferometer to verify that the surface meets industry standards for geometry and cleanliness.</p>
