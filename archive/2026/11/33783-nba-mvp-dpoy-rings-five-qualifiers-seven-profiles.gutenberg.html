Seven names, but only five complete the NBA's rarest three-part résumé: at least one regular-season Most Valuable Player award, one Defensive Player of the Year award, and one NBA championship. Checking the NBA's complete MVP history against its DPOY history produces five qualifiers through the 2025–26 season: Kevin Garnett, David Robinson, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Hakeem Olajuwon and Michael Jordan. Because only five players qualify, #7 and #6 are clearly labeled near-misses, while #5 through #1 feature all five qualifying players. At the reader's request, Hakeem Olajuwon ranks #5 and Kevin Garnett ranks #2. The ordering is editorial, not an official NBA award formula.

The ranking weighs the scale of career awards, championships, prime two-way influence and postseason leadership. It is an editorial judgment, not an official NBA classification. Regular-season MVP is different from Finals MVP. Awards need not arrive in the same season, and a player need not win every championship as his team's best performer. The DPOY award began in 1982–83, a crucial historical limitation for older legends such as Bill Russell.

#7. Kawhi Leonard: Two DPOYs and two rings, but no MVP

Near-miss, not a qualifier. Leonard won DPOY in 2015 and 2016, and championships with San Antonio in 2014 and Toronto in 2019. He also earned Finals MVP for both title runs. The missing achievement is a regular-season MVP; appearing in MVP discussions does not equal winning the award. His switchable defense and efficient half-court scoring make him an excellent illustration of how a postseason legend can miss the exact three-trophy test.

#6. Tim Duncan: Five championships and two MVPs, but no DPOY

Near-miss, not a qualifier. Duncan won regular-season MVP in 2002 and 2003, alongside five Spurs championships in 1999, 2003, 2005, 2007 and 2014. His positional defense, rebounding, rim protection and discipline made him one of the game's great defenders. But Duncan never won Defensive Player of the Year, despite sustained elite defensive recognition. The omission demonstrates why a formal award filter is not equivalent to a list of the greatest defenders.

#5. Hakeem Olajuwon: The most complete single-season sweep

Qualified. Olajuwon won DPOY in 1993 and 1994, the 1994 MVP and Houston's championships in 1994 and 1995. In the 1993–94 season he also became Finals MVP, producing the only same-season combination of MVP, DPOY, Finals MVP and a championship in league history. His footwork and scoring could overwhelm defenders, while his timing, rebounding and rim protection anchored Houston. Olajuwon's two DPOYs and two Finals MVPs establish an extraordinary two-way peak.

#4. David Robinson: A DPOY, an MVP and two championships

Qualified. Robinson's trophy sequence began with DPOY in 1992 and MVP in 1995, followed by Spurs titles in 1999 and 2003. The 7-foot-1 center supplied elite shot blocking, speed, scoring and transition defense. Robinson's two titles and historic defensive résumé earn him #4 in the requested countdown, although he shared San Antonio's title-winning frontcourt with Tim Duncan. USA Basketball's official player profile confirms all four dates and awards.

#3. Giannis Antetokounmpo: Two MVPs, DPOY and a title

Qualified. Antetokounmpo took MVP in both 2019 and 2020, DPOY in 2020 and the 2021 NBA championship. His 2020 season joins Jordan and Olajuwon as one of only three instances of MVP and DPOY in the same regular season. During Milwaukee's 2021 Finals clincher, Giannis added 50 points, 14 rebounds and five blocks. His two MVPs and central role on a championship team push him above Robinson, even though Robinson has one additional ring. The broader NBA player rankings provide a contemporary comparison.

#2. Kevin Garnett: 2004 MVP, 2008 DPOY and an NBA title

Qualified. Garnett captured regular-season MVP with Minnesota in 2004, then helped Boston win the 2008 NBA championship while claiming Defensive Player of the Year in the same season. His defensive communication, mobility, rebounding and playmaking made him a uniquely versatile frontcourt leader. The MVP came before his Boston championship, proving the three honors need not arrive together. The reader-requested countdown puts Garnett at #2 as an editorial ranking, not an award-based formula or an official NBA placement. The fuller argument appears in our Kevin Garnett career analysis.

#1. Michael Jordan: Five MVPs, a DPOY and six rings

Qualified, and first overall. Jordan won regular-season MVP in 1988, 1991, 1992, 1996 and 1998. He added DPOY in 1988, plus six Bulls championships and six Finals MVPs between 1991 and 1998. His 1987–88 season combined the league scoring title with MVP and DPOY, a rare display of offensive and defensive dominance by a guard. An unmatched six-for-six Finals MVP record among championship seasons, plus the highest MVP and title counts in the qualifying group, gives Jordan the top position.
