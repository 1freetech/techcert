<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>RJ45 Ethernet cabling</strong> and <strong>fiber optic cabling</strong> are two dominant physical media used in networking, each with unique characteristics suited for different scenarios. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>RJ45</strong> uses <strong>twisted-pair copper wires</strong> terminated with an <strong>8P8C modular connector</strong> (commonly referred to as RJ45) and is typically deployed in <strong>LAN environments</strong>. Ethernet standards like <strong>Cat5e, Cat6, and Cat6a</strong> support speeds from <strong>1 Gbps to 10 Gbps</strong> at distances up to <strong>100 meters</strong>, and RJ45 is favored for its <strong>cost-effectiveness</strong>, <strong>simplicity</strong>, and <strong>compatibility with PoE (Power over Ethernet)</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>However, copper cables are susceptible to <strong>EMI (electromagnetic interference)</strong> and signal loss over longer distances.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong>Fiber optic cables</strong>, by contrast, transmit data using <strong>light pulses</strong> through <strong>glass or plastic strands</strong>, offering much <strong>higher bandwidth</strong>, <strong>lower latency</strong>, and <strong>longer-distance transmission</strong>—up to <strong>40 km or more</strong> with single-mode fiber. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Fiber is immune to EMI, making it ideal for <strong>data centers, ISP backbones, and long-range point-to-point links</strong>. It also supports higher speeds like <strong>40 Gbps or 100 Gbps</strong> when used with technologies like <strong>QSFP or SFP+ modules</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>The trade-offs are <strong>higher cost</strong>, <strong>fragile connectors</strong>, and <strong>specialized tools</strong> required for termination and testing. For CompTIA A+ candidates, understanding when to use RJ45 vs fiber is essential for balancing performance, distance, and budget.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
