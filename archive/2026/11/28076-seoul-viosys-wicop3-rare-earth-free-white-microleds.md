<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>South Korea's Seoul Viosys and parent company Seoul Semiconductor announced on October 28, 2026, that they had begun manufacturing <strong>WICOP 3</strong>, a white-emitting micro-LED intended to eliminate the rare-earth phosphor layer and conventional package used in many white light-emitting diodes. Initial production devices measure <strong>25 × 10 micrometers</strong>, making the approach relevant to miniature displays, embedded illumination and potentially future optical links.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The companies' <a href="https://www.seoulviosys.com/en/media/news/212">primary announcement</a> says the chip produces white light from <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/31/infineon-1-1-billion-villach-300mm-gan-power-semiconductors/">gallium nitride (GaN)</a> without a separate rare-earth-containing phosphor conversion layer. The exact emission mechanism was not independently characterized in the release, so its device-level performance claims still need third-party testing.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Smaller white light, different trade-offs</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Conventional white LEDs frequently combine a blue semiconductor emitter with a phosphor that converts part of the light into longer wavelengths. WICOP 3 instead extends Seoul's package-free WICOP design into white output. This is distinct from <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/22/palomino-gan-microled-hyperscaler-order-ai-interconnects/">GaN micro-LEDs for AI optical interconnects</a>, where transmitting data and emitting useful white illumination are different performance requirements.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":28073,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} --><figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wicop3-background-real-led-die-bondwire-aircombat-cc0.jpg" alt="Real microscope photograph of a conventional surface-mount LED die with a bond wire, not a WICOP 3 chip" class="wp-image-28073" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em>Real conventional LED die and bondwire by <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:LED_Die_and_Bondwire.jpg">AirCombat / Wikimedia Commons</a>, CC0 1.0. Historical architecture example; <strong>not</strong> WICOP 3 hardware.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A historical photomicrograph of a conventional LED die helps explain why packaging and interconnects matter. It does not show the newly announced WICOP 3 structure.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Seoul reports <strong>0.015 lumens</strong> from its first 25 × 10 µm chip and luminous efficacy approaching <strong>100 lumens per watt</strong>. Its ambition is <strong>200 lm/W within two years</strong>, not an achieved measurement. Comparing the two requires matching operating conditions; a single tiny chip's light output also cannot be equated directly with the total output of a conventional lamp.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":28071,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} --><figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://bitcoinversus.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wicop3-efficacy-reported-2026-vs-company-2028-target-lm-per-watt.png" alt="Bar chart compares reported current WICOP 3 luminous efficacy near 100 lumens per watt with Seoul Viosys goal of 200 lumens per watt by 2028" class="wp-image-28071" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><em><a href="https://www.seoulviosys.com/en/media/news/212">Seoul Viosys</a> reported approximately 100 lm/W in October 2026; 200 lm/W is its goal within two years, not a tested result. Values are manufacturer-supplied.</em></figcaption></figure><!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Commercial questions remain</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Removing conversion materials and package components could simplify some manufacturing and improve material sourcing. The company claims production cost could be about half that of a conventional white LED, but provided no independently audited cost breakdown, shipment volume or customer design-win list.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Possible applications include <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/14/himax-always-on-vision-qwen-glasses/">AR eyewear</a>, thin lighting surfaces and optical communications. The last application should be evaluated against proven <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/optical-transceivers-complete-guide-sfp-qsfp-fiber-links-800g/">optical transceiver</a> requirements such as signal quality, power and distance, rather than broad data-rate promises.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Seoul says customer supply is planned for the fourth quarter of 2026. The decisive next evidence will be qualified volumes, measured efficiency and reliability, commercial prices, and actual customer deployments—not the much larger future-market projections in the announcement.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->
