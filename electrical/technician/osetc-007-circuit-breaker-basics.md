# OSETC.007: Circuit Breaker Basics

A **circuit breaker** is an electrical protection device. Its basic job is to interrupt current when an abnormal condition such as an overload or short circuit causes current to become unsafe for the protected circuit.

This technician lesson follows **OSETC.006: Electrical Power — Watts and Kilowatts**.

## Normal Operation

When the breaker is closed, current can flow. If its protection mechanism detects an overcurrent condition that exceeds its trip characteristics, it opens the circuit.

## Overload vs. Short Circuit

An **overload** occurs when a circuit carries more current than intended for too long. A **short circuit** is a fault that can produce very large current through an unintended low-resistance path.

## ON, OFF, and TRIPPED

- **ON:** breaker closed and intended to carry current.
- **OFF:** breaker intentionally opened.
- **TRIPPED:** protection mechanism operated.
- Exact handle indication varies by breaker design.

## Read the Nameplate

Depending on the breaker, markings may include current rating, voltage rating, number of poles, interrupting rating, model/family, and trip-unit information.

Never assume breakers are interchangeable because they look similar.

## Breaker vs. Fuse

Both can provide overcurrent protection. A fuse normally must be replaced after it operates. A breaker mechanically opens and may be reset only after the cause of the trip has been understood and the equipment is safe to re-energize.

## Data Center Example

A tripped upstream breaker is information, not permission to immediately reset it. Follow site electrical-safety procedures and investigate or escalate within your authorization and training.

## Safety Boundary

Do not remove panel covers, expose energized conductors, defeat interlocks, or perform live measurements unless qualified, authorized, properly equipped, and following site electrical-safety and lockout/tagout procedures.

## Practice

1. On de-energized training equipment or a photograph, identify the breaker handle.
2. Find the current rating.
3. Find voltage or other ratings.
4. Explain OFF versus TRIPPED.
5. Explain why the trip cause should be understood before reset.

## Key Takeaway

A circuit breaker is a protective switching device. Technician fundamentals are recognizing it, reading its ratings, understanding a trip, and respecting the electrical-safety boundary.
