# OSHistory.002: Commodity Money — Why Some Goods Work Better as Money

This lesson follows OSHistory.001 and explains why some physical goods repeatedly performed better as monetary media than others.

## Core outcomes

Learners should be able to define commodity money; explain the monetary importance of durability, portability, divisibility, recognizability, scarcity, acceptability, and relative stability; compare cattle, grain, salt, cowries, silver, and gold as monetary goods; explain how weighing, measurement, purity testing, storage, transport, and authentication technologies supported commodity money; explain why weighed metal created verification costs; and explain why coinage can be understood as a standardization technology.

## Historical caution

Commodity money is one important family of monetary arrangements, not a mandatory universal stage. Historical societies used many combinations of barter, commodities, credit, accounting, ledgers, obligations, coins, and other exchange systems.

## Key takeaway

Commodity money is a selection problem. Goods become stronger monetary media when they reduce the costs of storage, transport, division, verification, and exchange. The next major development in the sequence is standardized metal coinage.
