<p style="text-transform:none">The <strong>x64 architecture</strong>, commonly referred to as 64-bit computing, supports larger amounts of memory and improved computational capabilities compared to its predecessors. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">According to <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winprog64/the-advantages-of-64-bit-architecture">Microsoft</a>, x64 CPUs can address substantially more RAM, enhancing performance for memory-intensive tasks such as gaming, software development, and video editing. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jPvv-wZXpgo\u0026amp;t=154s","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Dominant in modern desktops and servers, x64 processors from companies like Intel and AMD are foundational for powerful, multitasking-oriented computing environments.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=su5mwn_dves\u0026amp;pp=ygUDeDY0","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <strong>x86 architecture</strong>, also known as 32-bit computing, originated from Intel's early processor designs. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">x86 CPUs are historically significant and primarily associated with compatibility and legacy software support. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hXZFgvIv11Y","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

</div></figure>
<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">While they have largely been replaced by x64 architectures due to memory limitations and performance bottlenecks, x86 processors remain relevant in specific scenarios, especially in embedded systems and legacy applications requiring backward compatibility.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">The <strong>ARM (Advanced RISC Machine) architecture</strong> emphasizes efficiency and low power consumption, making it ideal for mobile devices, tablets, and IoT solutions. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_R06xdksYTI","type":"video","providerNameSlug":"youtube","responsive":true,"className":"wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"} -->

</div></figure>
<!-- /wp:embed -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">According to <a>Arm Holdings</a>, ARM processors utilize a simplified instruction set (RISC), significantly reducing energy usage while maintaining adequate processing power. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"style":{"typography":{"textTransform":"none"}}} -->
<p style="text-transform:none">Due to these advantages, ARM CPUs power most smartphones, tablets, wearables, and increasingly, data centers and laptops, highlighting their versatility and growing market influence.</p>
