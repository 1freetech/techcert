<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Why The MOS Capacitor Matters</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/01/14/semiconductor-fundamentals-metal-oxide-semiconductor-mos/"><strong>metal–oxide–semiconductor (MOS)</strong></a> capacitor is the electrostatic core of the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/25/mosfet-operating-characteristics/"><strong>MOSFET</strong></a>. Its stack is simple: a conductive gate, a thin insulating oxide, and a semiconductor substrate. The oxide blocks steady DC current while still allowing the gate electric field to rearrange charge near the silicon surface. For a simple parallel-plate approximation, the oxide capacitance is <strong>C<sub>ox</sub> = ε<sub>ox</sub>A/t<sub>ox</sub></strong>, or per unit area <strong>C′<sub>ox</sub> = ε<sub>ox</sub>/t<sub>ox</sub></strong>. A thinner oxide therefore produces a larger gate capacitance and stronger electrostatic control. This lesson extends the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/ossec-001-semiconductor-device-physics-band-gaps-doping-pn-junctions/"><strong>band-gap and doping physics</strong></a>, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/ossec-002-carrier-transport-drift-diffusion-mobility-recombination/"><strong>carrier transport</strong></a>, and <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/ossec-003-pn-junction-electrostatics-depletion-width-built-in-potential-electric-field-junction-capacitance/"><strong>electrostatics</strong></a> developed earlier in the track.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Accumulation Pulls Majority Carriers To The Surface</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Consider an NMOS-style capacitor built on p-type silicon. A sufficiently negative gate voltage attracts holes, the majority carriers, toward the oxide–silicon interface. That region is called <strong>accumulation</strong>. The surface becomes more heavily populated with holes than the neutral bulk, and the small-signal capacitance approaches the oxide capacitance because there is no wide depletion region acting in series with the oxide. The sign reverses for an n-type substrate. Thinking in terms of charge is useful, but the same behavior can also be read from the energy-band bending inherited from <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/ossec-003-pn-junction-electrostatics-depletion-width-built-in-potential-electric-field-junction-capacitance/"><strong>semiconductor electrostatics</strong></a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Depletion Creates A Second Effective Capacitor</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>As the gate on a p-type substrate moves positive, holes are repelled from the surface and leave behind fixed ionized acceptors. The resulting space-charge region is <strong>depletion</strong>. Under the depletion approximation, its width grows roughly as <strong>W<sub>d</sub> ≈ √(2ε<sub>si</sub>ψ<sub>s</sub> / qN<sub>A</sub>)</strong>, where ψ<sub>s</sub> is surface potential and N<sub>A</sub> is acceptor density. The depletion region has its own capacitance, <strong>C′<sub>dep</sub> = ε<sub>si</sub>/W<sub>d</sub></strong>. The measured MOS capacitance therefore behaves like oxide and depletion capacitances in series: <strong>1/C′ = 1/C′<sub>ox</sub> + 1/C′<sub>dep</sub></strong>. This is closely related to the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/05/ossec-003-pn-junction-electrostatics-depletion-width-built-in-potential-electric-field-junction-capacitance/"><strong>depletion-width and junction-capacitance</strong></a> reasoning used for PN junctions.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Inversion Creates The Future MOSFET Channel</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>Continue increasing the positive gate voltage and the p-type surface eventually becomes electron-rich. Minority carriers now outnumber holes at the interface, producing an <strong>inversion layer</strong>. In the strong-inversion picture, the surface potential is commonly approximated as <strong>ψ<sub>s</sub> ≈ 2φ<sub>F</sub></strong>, where the magnitude of the bulk Fermi potential for p-type material is <strong>φ<sub>F</sub> = (kT/q) ln(N<sub>A</sub>/n<sub>i</sub>)</strong>. The depletion region then approaches a maximum width while additional gate voltage primarily increases inversion charge. Once source and drain regions are added, this controllable electron sheet becomes the conductive channel that defines <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/25/mosfet-operating-characteristics/"><strong>MOSFET operation</strong></a>.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Threshold Voltage Combines Surface Potential And Oxide Drop</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/27/semiconductor-physics-threshold-voltage/"><strong>threshold voltage</strong></a> is the gate voltage associated with the onset of strong inversion. For an ideal NMOS capacitor on p-type silicon, a common engineering form is <strong>V<sub>T</sub> ≈ V<sub>FB</sub> + 2φ<sub>F</sub> + |Q′<sub>d,max</sub>|/C′<sub>ox</sub></strong>. The first term is the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/01/16/flat-band-capacitance/"><strong>flat-band</strong></a> reference, the second supplies the required silicon band bending, and the third is the oxide voltage required to support maximum depletion charge. Real devices shift because of gate work-function differences, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/01/15/semiconductor-physics-oxide-charges/"><strong>oxide charge</strong></a>, interface states, substrate bias, and other non-idealities. That is why <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/03/03/understanding-threshold-voltage-extraction-for-rf-mosfets/"><strong>threshold-voltage extraction</strong></a> is a measurement problem as well as a theory problem.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">The C–V Curve Turns Electrostatics Into A Measurement</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/22/metal-oxide-semiconductor-capacitance-voltage-mos-cv/"><strong>MOS C–V measurement</strong></a> sweeps DC gate bias while applying a small AC signal to measure incremental capacitance. In accumulation, C approaches C<sub>ox</sub>. In depletion, the widening space-charge region lowers total capacitance. In inversion, the result depends on measurement frequency: at low enough frequency, minority carriers can respond and capacitance may rise again toward C<sub>ox</sub>; at high frequency, inversion charge cannot follow the AC signal quickly enough, so capacitance stays near a minimum set by oxide and maximum depletion capacitance in series. C–V data can therefore reveal oxide thickness, doping, <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/23/extracting-flat-band-capacitance-cfb/"><strong>flat-band behavior</strong></a>, threshold, and signs of <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/02/24/oxide-charge-density-in-semiconductors/"><strong>oxide charge density</strong></a> or interface problems.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Read Band Diagrams And Charge Together</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph --><p>A reliable MOS analysis uses three views at the same time: gate voltage, charge distribution, and energy bands. Negative gate bias on p-type silicon produces accumulation; modest positive bias produces depletion; larger positive bias bends the bands far enough to create inversion. The <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/ossec-001-semiconductor-device-physics-band-gaps-doping-pn-junctions/"><strong>Fermi-level and doping concepts</strong></a> from OSSEC.001 explain why carrier concentrations change exponentially with band position, while the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/10/04/ossec-002-carrier-transport-drift-diffusion-mobility-recombination/"><strong>carrier-transport framework</strong></a> explains why minority carriers may or may not respond quickly enough during AC measurements. The central engineering habit is to avoid memorizing a C–V curve in isolation: derive what should happen from field direction, charge sign, band bending, and available carriers.</p><!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Worked Example</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list --><ul class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>Given:</strong> SiO₂ thickness t<sub>ox</sub> = 10 nm and ε<sub>ox</sub> ≈ 3.45 × 10<sup>−11</sup> F/m.</li><li><strong>Convert thickness:</strong> 10 nm = 1.0 × 10<sup>−8</sup> m.</li><li><strong>Oxide capacitance per unit area:</strong> C′<sub>ox</sub> = ε<sub>ox</sub>/t<sub>ox</sub>.</li><li><strong>Result:</strong> C′<sub>ox</sub> ≈ 3.45 × 10<sup>−3</sup> F/m².</li><li><strong>Equivalent:</strong> about 3.45 fF/µm².</li><li><strong>Engineering meaning:</strong> halving oxide thickness approximately doubles C′<sub>ox</sub>, strengthening gate electrostatic coupling before quantum and reliability effects are considered.</li></ul><!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading --><h2 class="wp-block-heading">Knowledge Check + Answers</h2><!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} --><ol class="wp-block-list"><li><strong>What is C′<sub>ox</sub>?</strong> Oxide capacitance per unit area, approximately ε<sub>ox</sub>/t<sub>ox</sub>.</li><li><strong>What is accumulation?</strong> An increase of majority carriers at the semiconductor surface.</li><li><strong>What is depletion?</strong> A surface region depleted of mobile majority carriers and containing fixed ionized dopants.</li><li><strong>What is inversion?</strong> A surface condition where minority carriers become the dominant mobile carrier type.</li><li><strong>What approximately defines strong inversion for an ideal p-type substrate?</strong> ψ<sub>s</sub> ≈ 2φ<sub>F</sub>.</li><li><strong>Why does MOS capacitance fall in depletion?</strong> Oxide capacitance and depletion capacitance act in series.</li><li><strong>Why can high-frequency inversion capacitance stay low?</strong> Minority carriers cannot respond rapidly enough to the AC test signal.</li></ol><!-- /wp:list -->
