<p><strong>OSHE.008</strong> examines a remarkable surveying achievement: the Great Pyramid of Khufu was laid out almost exactly along Earth’s north–south and east–west directions. A monument roughly 230 meters across needs consistent reference lines before most stone blocks are placed. Following <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/21/oshe-007-khufu-merer-diary-limestone-transport-giza-workers/">OSHE.007 on moving limestone to Giza</a>, the lesson examines how builders could establish a dependable orientation without modern instruments.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What surviving stones tell surveyors</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>True north</strong> follows the geographical meridian toward Earth’s rotational pole. <strong>Magnetic north</strong> points toward the shifting magnetic pole. Neither a modern compass nor satellite positioning was necessary to observe the sky. Researchers measuring surviving casing-related traces report orientation differences of only a few <strong>arcminutes</strong>; one arcminute equals one-sixtieth of a degree. Glen Dash’s <a href="https://www.glendash.com/archaeology/as-published.html">survey research</a> summarizes an error under four arcminutes. Modern teams use measured foundations and original casing evidence, not just eroded outer faces; the <a href="https://aeraweb.org/projects/gpmp/">Ancient Egypt Research Associates survey program</a> documents that work.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Solar shadows versus northern stars</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Solar-shadow hypothesis:</strong> a gnomon is a rod casting a movable shadow. On a properly leveled surface, observers could mark successive shadow-tip positions. Around an equinox, carefully interpreted observations may help establish an east–west direction; a perpendicular line defines north–south. Dash tested an equinoctial-shadow method and found a plausible explanation for the alignment error. Weather, timing, rod placement, and imperfect measurements affect results. Demonstrating feasibility does not establish that Khufu’s workers used the method.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Stellar hypothesis:</strong> observers could watch circumpolar stars and transfer the inferred meridian to ground markers. Astronomer Kate Spence proposed a <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/35042510">simultaneous transit of two northern stars</a>. Stellar techniques depend on accurate sighting and the changing orientation of the celestial pole across centuries. <a href="https://www.si.edu/spotlight/ancient-egypt/pyramid">Smithsonian’s account</a> describes astronomy as important to pyramid orientation, but surviving records do not uniquely identify an original surveying procedure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Turn a north–south line into four accurate sides</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A <strong>baseline</strong> provides a reference direction. Surveyors can construct a perpendicular east–west axis, repeat the desired side lengths, and check corner geometry. In a classroom, sides of <strong>3, 4, and 5 units</strong> produce a right angle: 3² + 4² = 5². A square with side L has diagonals of length <strong>L√2</strong>. Equal diagonals alone do not prove an arbitrary quadrilateral is square; consistent side lengths and right angles also matter. Rope geometry illustrates mathematical possibilities, not proof of historical tools. Compare the transition from <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/01/oshe-004-meidum-pyramid-stepped-core-true-pyramid-sneferu-engineering/">Meidum</a> to <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/07/oshe-005-sneferu-bent-pyramid-dahshur-changing-slope-engineering/">Sneferu’s Bent Pyramid</a> and the <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2026/11/18/oshe-006-sneferu-red-pyramid-dahshur-geometry-masonry-true-pyramid/">Red Pyramid</a>.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How far does an error of three arcminutes reach?</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Worked example:</strong> three arcminutes equals 3/60 = <strong>0.05°</strong>. Across an assumed 230-meter reference line, the lateral offset from an ideal meridian is <strong>230 × tan(0.05°) ≈ 0.20 m</strong>, or about 20 centimeters. The number illustrates angular precision; it is <em>not</em> an observed misplaced pyramid corner. Archaeologists instead fit lines to multiple preserved points and assess measurement uncertainty. <a href="https://www.pbs.org/video/decoding-the-great-pyramid-4eeiz9/">PBS NOVA’s “Decoding the Great Pyramid”</a> follows Dash’s field survey and proposed shadow experiment.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Practice questions and answers</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Question:</strong> What is the diagonal of a square foundation five meters wide? <strong>Answer:</strong> 5√2 ≈ 7.07 meters. <strong>Question:</strong> Does a small azimuth error prove an equinox-shadow method? <strong>Answer:</strong> No; several proposals are consistent with comparable accuracy. <strong>Question:</strong> Does the calculated 20-centimeter offset represent an actual defect? <strong>Answer:</strong> No; it depends on an assumed angle and distance.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Lesson conclusion:</strong> pyramid alignment combines astronomy, measurement, geometry, and construction control. Preserved evidence reveals remarkable precision, while the exact ancient technique remains disputed. <em>The cover illustration is a fictional reconstruction rather than evidence of particular workers or tools.</em></p>
