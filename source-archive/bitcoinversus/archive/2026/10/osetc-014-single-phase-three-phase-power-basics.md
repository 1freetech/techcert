# OSETC.014: Single-Phase and Three-Phase Power Basics

Single-phase and three-phase describe two common ways AC electrical power is delivered.

## Single-Phase Power

Single-phase AC uses one alternating voltage waveform. It is common for everyday residential and light-commercial loads such as lighting, receptacles, computers, and small appliances.

## Three-Phase Power

Three-phase AC uses three alternating waveforms separated by 120 electrical degrees. It is common for larger motors, pumps, cooling equipment, industrial machinery, and data-center distribution.

## Simple Mental Model

One evenly repeating push is a useful picture for single phase. Three evenly spaced pushes are a useful picture for how three phases can provide smoother power transfer.

## Where Technicians See Them

- Single-phase: homes, offices, ordinary receptacles, lighting, and smaller equipment.
- Three-phase: large motors, pumps, chillers, industrial equipment, distribution gear, and many data-center loads.

## Data Center Example

A data center may receive three-phase power for facility loads and distribution equipment while individual devices may operate from a single phase or converted DC power. Always identify the equipment rating and approved connection.

## Bitcoin Mining Example

Large mining facilities can distribute substantial loads across three-phase systems. Individual power supplies and miners must still be connected according to rated input voltage and the engineered distribution design.

## Safety Boundary

Recognition of phases is not authorization to open energized equipment or take live measurements. Follow site procedures, equipment ratings, lockout/tagout, PPE, and qualified-person boundaries.

## Key Takeaway

Single-phase uses one AC waveform. Three-phase uses three waveforms separated by 120 degrees and is common for larger power loads. Technician fundamentals are recognition, correct equipment identification, and safe work boundaries.
