OSETC.002 introduces safe digital multimeter use and voltage measurement. A meter is one of an electrical technician’s most useful tools, but selecting the wrong function, input jack, or equipment rating can create a serious hazard.

## Inspect before measuring
Before using a digital multimeter, inspect the meter, display, selector, probes, and test-lead insulation. Damaged equipment should be removed from service. The meter and accessories must also be appropriately rated for the circuit being tested.

## Know the basic connections
For a typical voltage measurement, the black lead connects to **COM** and the red lead connects to the voltage input marked **V**. Select AC voltage for alternating-current systems and DC voltage for direct-current systems. Never assume the lead position or selector setting is correct simply because the meter was used previously.

## Understand CAT ratings
Measurement-category ratings describe the transient environment for which a meter is designed. A voltage number alone does not tell the whole safety story. Technicians should verify that the meter and probes are suitable for the electrical system and work location before making contact.

## Voltage is measured across two points
A voltage measurement compares electrical potential between two points. Do not probe unfamiliar energized equipment merely to practice. Training measurements should be performed on an appropriate low-energy training circuit or under the procedures and supervision required by the workplace.

## Live-dead-live verification
When verifying absence of voltage, establish that the test instrument itself is functioning. Verify it on a known energized source, test the circuit expected to be de-energized, and verify the instrument again on the known source.

## Technician exercise
1. With the meter disconnected from a circuit, identify COM, voltage, and current input jacks.
2. Find the meter’s CAT and voltage ratings.
3. Inspect both test leads for damaged insulation or probe tips.
4. Identify AC-voltage, DC-voltage, resistance, and continuity selector positions.
5. On an appropriate safe training source, practice selecting the correct voltage function and reading the display.

## OSET takeaway
A technician should be able to inspect a meter, identify its ratings, select the correct input and function, understand what a voltage measurement represents, and follow the required safety procedure before trusting a measurement.
