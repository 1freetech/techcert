<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/24/mac-address-media-access-control-address/">IPv4</a> (Internet Protocol version 4)</strong> is the fourth version of the IP protocol and remains the most widely used standard for assigning <a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/04/10/static-ip-vs-dhcp/">IP addresses</a> on the internet and private networks. It uses a <strong>32-bit addressing scheme</strong>, represented in dotted decimal format (e.g., <code>192.168.1.1</code>), which provides approximately <strong>4.3 billion unique addresses</strong>. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>Due to the limited pool of IPv4 addresses, techniques like <strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/25/comparing-and-contrasting-common-network-configuration-concepts/">NAT (Network Address Translation)</a></strong> and <strong>CIDR (Classless Inter-Domain Routing)</strong> were implemented to conserve space and optimize routing. IPv4 is easy to implement and widely supported, but it faces ongoing limitations due to <strong>address exhaustion</strong> and growing device connectivity.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p><strong><a href="https://bitcoinversus.tech/2025/03/30/ipv6/">IPv6</a> (Internet Protocol version 6)</strong> was developed to address IPv4’s limitations by introducing a <strong>128-bit address format</strong>, allowing for <strong>340 undecillion unique addresses</strong>—more than enough to support the future of IoT and global networking. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph {"className":""} -->
<p>IPv6 addresses are written in <strong>hexadecimal notation</strong> and separated by colons (e.g., <code>2001:0db8:85a3::8a2e:0370:7334</code>). Unlike IPv4, IPv6 natively supports <strong>auto-configuration, multicast, and improved security with IPsec</strong>. It eliminates the need for NAT, streamlining end-to-end connectivity. Transition technologies such as <strong>dual stack</strong> and <strong>tunneling</strong> help bridge IPv4 and IPv6 environments during the global transition to IPv6 infrastructure.</p>
